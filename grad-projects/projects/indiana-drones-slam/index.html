Indiana Drones: SLAM in the forest

A drone lands somewhere in a forest with no map and no GPS. It can only measure distance and bearing to the trees it can see. It has to build a map, keep track of itself within that map, and fly to a treasure without hitting anything.

The problem

The drone treats its landing spot as (0, 0). Each step it gets measurements to the trees within its sensing horizon, then issues a move (turn, then fly forward). Moves are noisy, so trusting the commands alone (dead reckoning) drifts further off every step.

Part A was pure SLAM: process a given sequence of moves and measurements, then report the drone's and every tree's position. Part B added planning: reach the treasure and extract it from within 0.25 m, without crashing into a tree.

My approach

  • Online Graph SLAM: I kept an information matrix Ω and vector ξ over the drone's pose and every tree seen so far. Each measurement adds a constraint between the drone and a tree, and a new tree grows the matrix by two rows and columns.
  • Marginalizing old poses: after every move I inserted the new pose, linked it to the old one with the motion constraint, then folded the old pose away with the Schur complement (Ω′ = Ω − AᵀB⁻¹A). The matrix only grows with the number of trees, not with the number of steps.
  • Estimate: μ = Ω⁻¹ξ gives the best guess for the drone and the whole map at once.
  • Planner: fly straight at the treasure unless a mapped tree (using its measured radius plus a buffer) blocks the line. If one does, aim at a tangent point just outside it on the side closer to the goal. Steering and distance are clamped to the drone's limits.

What to try

Switch to "Dead reckoning only." The drone flies confidently to where it thinks the treasure is and misses. SLAM uses the trees as reference points to correct its drift.
