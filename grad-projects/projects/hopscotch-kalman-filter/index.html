HopScotch: hopping across asteroids

A small ship has to get home by jumping from asteroid to asteroid. Its sensors only give noisy positions. To land safely it needs to know where each rock will be next, not where it seems to be now.

The problem

Dozens of asteroids drift across the screen, each with its own velocity and acceleration. Every step the ship gets a noisy (x, y) reading for each one. The project had two parts: predict every asteroid's position one step into the future, then use those predictions to choose jumps that carry the ship to the top without missing a landing.

My approach

  • One filter per asteroid: a Kalman filter is created the first time an asteroid is seen and dropped when it disappears, so the tracker handles rocks coming and going.
  • Constant-acceleration model: a 6-D state of position, velocity and acceleration. The transition matrix includes the ½·a terms, and the measurement matrix picks out position only.
  • Tuning: I set a large initial covariance so unknown velocity and acceleration are learned quickly from the first few readings, and set the measurement noise to match the sensor.
  • Jump policy: I only jumped to asteroids within about 77% of the maximum jump distance, as a safety margin. Candidates had to be higher than the current rock and, based on the filter's velocity estimate, moving upward and toward the center of the field.

Why it matters

The HUD compares prediction error: the Kalman estimate is typically less than half the error of the raw reading, because it averages out the noise and accounts for motion. That margin is the difference between landing and falling.
