Warehouse: delivery robot

A robot has to bring numbered boxes back to a dropzone, in order, through a cluttered warehouse. First with A* search, then with a full policy that still works when the robot's wheels slip.

The problem

The robot moves in eight directions (straight moves are cheaper than diagonal), lifts a box from an adjacent square, and sets it down on the dropzone from an adjacent square. It can't set a box down while standing on the dropzone. The project had three parts:

  • Part A: plan the full sequence of moves to deliver the boxes in order, in a warehouse the robot can only see around itself.
  • Part B: produce a complete policy (best action from every square) when each floor tile has its own cost.
  • Part C: the same, but moves are stochastic. The robot sometimes slips 45° or 90° off its intended direction.

My approach

  • A* per leg: each trip (dropzone → box, box → dropzone) is its own A* search over the 8-connected grid with a Chebyshev-distance heuristic. The goal test is "next to the target," not "on it."
  • Only what it can see: in part A the robot only learns what a cell contains when it's next to it, so I cached every observed cell and planned with what was known so far.
  • Path to actions: I walked the search tree backward from the goal to rebuild the path, then added lift and down actions. The robot always steps off the dropzone before putting a box down.
  • Value iteration (B and C): starting from the goal, I repeatedly updated each cell's cost-to-go until nothing changed. In part C each update is an expected cost over the intended, slanted and sideways outcomes, and a collision costs a big penalty. The state also tracked whether the robot is carrying a box.

What to try

In "Stochastic policy," drag "Moves as intended" down to 0.4. The arrows start steering away from walls, because a slip next to a wall means a costly collision. Click a box to retarget, or click the floor to add or remove shelving.
