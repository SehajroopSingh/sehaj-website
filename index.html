How do you get AI to work inside the systems — and budgets — people already have?

Most AI demos fall apart when they meet a real enterprise stack, a real budget or a real user. My work lives in that gap — in three ways: building the AI systems themselves, integrating them into the environments they have to survive in, and communicating with the people who use them so the work keeps getting better.

01 Build.

Agentic LLM pipelines, RAG, evaluation and guardrails — production systems that stay reliable, fast and affordable.

02 Integrate.

Fitting AI and data systems into the stacks, compliance rules and workflows a business already runs on.

03 Communicate.

Work only matters when people can use it. Without communication there’s no feedback, without feedback there’s no iteration, and without iteration nothing gets better or gets used. I explain complex work in plain terms to students, faculty and executives, listen, and iterate until real people can rely on it alongside real systems.

Running through all three: Generative AI · Enterprise systems · Regulated environments · Education — the four settings this work happens in, in different combinations on every project.

How I work

The loop I run on every project

AI that survives production isn’t built once — it’s measured into existence. Six steps, in order, and then again.

Where it has run J&J MedTech — steps 1–4, FDA-regulated MemSurf — all six, 0 → 99%+ reliable FotonVR — steps 3–5, Graph-RAG

↻and again
Step 01 Analyze the workflow Shadow the job. Trace where time and errors actually go.
Step 02 Understand the need A metric, a baseline and a counter-metric — agreed with the users.
Step 03 Build the gold standard Real inputs, ideal outputs, hand-checked — before any model.
Step 04 Apply AI The smallest system that works: prompt → RAG → agents, inside the existing stack.
Step 05 Test against the gold standard Score every change on quality, latency and cost. Guardrail what still fails.
Step 06 Ship, measure, iterate Production failures feed the gold set. Run the loop again.

Analyze the workflow; understand the need; build the gold standard; apply AI; test against the gold standard; ship, measure and iterate — then repeat.

Why I build the way I do

I’m a product of the California Community Colleges.

Santa Monica College got me to UC Santa Barbara, and from there to a Master’s in Computer Science at Georgia Tech. Before any of that, I spent a year teaching English in Chiapas, Mexico, to students whose resources looked nothing like mine.

Cost engineering is access engineering: every cent saved per AI call is a learner who doesn’t have to pay.

That’s why MemSurf is free, ad-supported and multilingual, and why I care as much about what an AI system costs and how reliably it runs as about what it can do. It’s also why I teach: the people who most need these tools are often the last to get them.
